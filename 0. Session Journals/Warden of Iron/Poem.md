---

Standing atop a lonely tower,
beneath the memory of the sun.
An elf of silver armor: Ilrien Stargazer.
A ghost remembering his own last breath.
Below, the forest burns,
a black ocean of banners unknown
chanting praise to Warren Drassig
whose shadow stretches through time itself.

He tells us:
"This tower should never have fallen.
We were betrayed from within."
His voice hums like leyline static,
and the conduit beside him likewise with ruin.
Once a bridge to the world’s channels,
now silent, its pulse stolen.

---

We descend.
Elven names whisper like echoes
through corridors of dust:
Ayre. Elen. Nuovis. Paeral.
Vulen. Luvon. Dakath.
Shyrrik. Illitran. Arnarra.
The roll of the dead and the lost.
So we assume.

Blood stains the wall where Lusha died,
her shadow refusing to rest.
Two mourners seal their tongues with silence.
We follow the shimmer of unseen energy,
and the room breathes wrong.
The explosion came from within the leyline
as if the earth itself coughed out a secret
Too heavy to keep.

---

We hunt for the Vein Tenders,
those who cradle the arteries of the world.
Shyrrik Avarren greets us with calm deceit.
She speaks of trouble below,
of Arnarra’s voice leading her astray,
of Illitran’s presence where he should not be.
She speaks carefully, as if measuring how much of the truth
we can survive. We believe it.

The new recruit, Nym Northyra,
remembers nothing but fear.
Procedures abandoned,
rooms disturbed,
a wand of broken memory left behind
like a conscience snapped in half.

---

In the infirmary,
the Wardens wait to die.
None of the bodies cast shadows.
Vulen, pale and fading,
cannot remember the road he bled upon.
Something hollow wore their faces
on the march home.
Something colder than death.

Below, the dread thickens.
Luceran is thrown back in a blast.
Shadows pulled into a dodecahedron of black despair.
Inside: a heart.
Dried but still beating in magic’s pulse.
Later, the name Nuovis carved in torn cloth.
Wretched from an undead thrall.
The forge-master speaks:
“Only Ilrien, Shyrrik, and the dead Lucia
could shape such ore.”

---

We find Illitran behind a locked door,
his mind gone,
his flesh a marionette of shadow.
Arnarra’s dispelling light pierces the veil,
and memory floods back.
She saw him cold,
his breath stolen by another’s will.
The name returns:
Anvaeryn.
The betrayers.
The family that fled into the dark
to kneel before the Elemental Lords.

Shyrrik Avarren. Shyrrik Anvaeryn.
The saboteur, the seed of corruption,
the shadow that killed the tower.

---

When all is done,
Ilrien’s projection flickers like candlelight.
He asks us to carry his final truth:
“The Order may perish.
Our knowledge may burn.
But the veins of the world must not break.
The filter must hold.”

He speaks of the ancient plan:
to unseal the banished lords,
to draw their essence into the world once more
through leyline, malgrite, and divine deceit.
To bend the earth toward shadow.
To make the material tremble.

We stand amid dust and memory.
The tower sleeps again.
But the veins still hum beneath our feet,
and the heart of betrayal
has not yet stopped beating.
